1–2Preliminary

1Name of the company

The name of the company is The Serendib Endowment Ltd.

2Type of company

The company is a not-for-profit public company limited by guarantee, established to operate as a registered charity.

3–6Definitions and interpretations

3Definitions

In this constitution:

ACNC Act means the Australian Charities and Not-for-profits Commission Act 2012 (Cth).

business day means a day on which banks are open for business, other than a Saturday, Sunday or public holiday in the location of the company’s registered office.

company means the company referred to in clause 1.

Corporations Act means the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth).

Corpus means the capital assets of the endowment fund, comprising all gifts, donations, bequests, and other contributions received by the company, together with any capital growth on those assets, but excluding income and returns designated by the directors for distribution in a financial year.

chairperson means an individual elected by the directors to be the company’s chairperson under clause 40.

general meeting means a meeting of members, including an annual general meeting.

members present means, in connection with a general meeting, members present in person, present by use of audio- or audio-visual technology, by representative or by proxy at the meeting.

members’ resolution has the meaning given by clause 31.1(a).

members’ statement has the meaning given by clause 31.1(c).

registered charity means a charity registered under the ACNC Act.

representative means a person appointed to represent an incorporated member in accordance with clause 27.

special resolution means a resolution:

  1. (a)of which notice has been given under clause 24.3(d), and
  2. (b)that has been passed by at least 75% of the votes cast by members present and entitled to vote on the resolution.

surplus assets means any company assets that remain after paying all the company’s debts and other liabilities, including the costs of winding up.

virtual meeting platform means any technology or combination of technologies that allows members to participate in a meeting, including by asking questions verbally and in writing, without being physically present at the meeting.

4Reading this constitution with the Corporations Act

  1. (a)The replaceable rules set out in the Corporations Act do not apply to the company.
  2. (b)While the company is a registered charity, the ACNC Act and the Corporations Act override any clauses in this constitution that are inconsistent with those Acts, as they apply to a registered charity.
  3. (c)If the company is not a registered charity (even if it remains charitable), the Corporations Act overrides any clause in this constitution which is inconsistent with that Act.
  4. (d)Unless otherwise stated, a word or expression that is defined in the Corporations Act or used in that Act and covering the same subject has the same meaning as in this constitution.

5Signing documents

Where the constitution says that an individual (such as a member, representative or director) must sign a document, the individual may:

  1. (a)sign a physical form of the document by hand, or
  2. (b)sign an electronic form of the document using an electronic signature, in a way that identifies each person and indicates their intention.

Individuals may also sign separate copies of a physical or electronic document if each copy has the same wording.

6Interpretation

In this constitution:

  1. (a)the words ‘including’, ‘for example’, or similar expressions mean that there may be more inclusions or examples than those mentioned after that expression, and
  2. (b)reference to an Act includes every amendment, re-enactment, or replacement of that Act and any subordinate legislation made under that Act (such as regulations).

7–10Charitable purposes and powers

7Purpose

The company’s purposes are to:

relieve poverty, distress, and disadvantage among individuals and communities in Sri Lanka, by providing grants, financial support, goods, and services to individuals in need and to organisations working to relieve poverty in Sri Lanka;

advance the education of individuals in Sri Lanka who lack access to educational opportunity, by providing scholarships, grants, financial support, educational materials, and goods to enable those individuals to obtain an education;

relieve the suffering of individuals and communities in Sri Lanka in times of natural disaster or other emergency, by providing grants, financial support, goods, and essential services to those affected; and

advance the purposes in (a) to (c) above by operating a charitable endowment fund, investing its assets, and making grants to individuals and organisations with purposes consistent with those above.

8Powers

Solely for carrying out the company’s purposes, the company has all the powers of a company limited by guarantee under the Corporations Act.

9Not-for-profit

The income and assets of the company must be applied solely to carry out its purpose(s) as stated in clause 7.

The company must not distribute any income or assets directly or indirectly to its members, except as provided in clause 82 or the following:

  1. (a)paying a member for goods or services they have provided, or expenses they have properly incurred at fair and reasonable rates or rates more favourable to the company, or
  2. (b)making a distribution of income or assets to a member in furtherance of the company’s charitable purpose(s).

10Amending the constitution

  1. (a)The members may amend this constitution by passing a special resolution. However, if the members pass a special resolution to amend the constitution in a way that means the company would no longer be a charity, the special resolution will not take effect.
  2. (b)The company must notify the ACNC of any amendments to this constitution.
  3. (c)Any amendment to this constitution will take effect from the date of the special resolution, or from any later date specified in the resolution.

11–18Members

11Membership

There will be one class of members.

The members of the company are those:

  1. (a)listed on the application for incorporation of the company with their consent, and
  2. (b)any other person admitted as a member, in accordance with this constitution,

and have not since stopped being a member.

12Register of members

The secretary must keep a register of members which includes the following information:

  1. (a)for each current member:
    1. i.name, including the name of any representative of an incorporated member
    2. ii.an email address or postal address nominated by the member for receiving notices, and
    3. iii.date the membership started, and
  2. (b)for each person who stopped being a member in the last seven years:
    1. i.name, including the name of any representative of an incorporated member
    2. ii.an email address or postal address nominated by the member for receiving notices, and
    3. iii.dates the membership started and ended.

13How to become a member

13.1Eligibility to become a member

Any person at least 18 years old who, or any incorporated body which, supports the purposes of the company and is nominated in writing by two existing members may apply in writing and pay any joining fee to become a member of the company.

13.2Applications for membership

  1. (a)The directors must consider each application for membership within a reasonable period after it is received and decide whether to accept or refuse the application.
  2. (b)The company must provide notice to the applicant about the decision to accept or refuse the application but are not required to provide reasons.
  3. (c)If the application is accepted, the secretary must add the applicant to the register of members. The date the applicant was added to the register of members is the day they became a member.

14Members’ rights and opportunities

A member can:

  1. (a)attend and vote at general meetings
  2. (b)raise a dispute using clause 19
  3. (c)together with other members, ask the directors to call a general meeting, under clause 22
  4. (d)together with other members, put forward resolutions at general meetings, under clause 31.1
  5. (e)be made a director (if the member is eligible to be a director) under clause 36
  6. (f)inspect the members’ register free of charge under clause 70, and
  7. (g)inspect the company’s records under clause 71.

15Members’ responsibilities

A member must:

  1. (a)comply with the company’s constitution
  2. (b)notify the company if they change their name or address for notices
  3. (c)treat other members, staff, volunteers, and directors with respect, and
  4. (d)pay membership fees (if any) and provide the guarantee specified in clause 83.

16Transfer of membership

Members cannot transfer their membership or their rights as members to another individual or incorporated body.

17Membership fees

17.1Directors may set fees

  1. (a)The directors may decide to set or change a joining fee, annual membership fee, or both, and the due date for payment.
  2. (b)The company must give members at least one month’s notice of any new fees or changes to fees.

17.2Failure to pay fees

  1. (a)If a member fails to pay the fees within two months of them becoming due, the company must notify that member and provide one month to rectify the default. If the member fails to rectify the default, then the member stops being a member.
  2. (b)A member that has not paid the required membership fee may not exercise any of the rights associated with their membership, including the right to exercise any vote they may have at a meeting of members.

18How to stop being a member

A member immediately stops being a member if they:

  1. (a)default on payment of the membership fee in accordance with clause 17 17
  2. (b)resign, by writing to the secretary
  3. (c)have not responded within one month to a written request from the secretary to confirm in writing that they want to remain a member
  4. (d)are an incorporated member and are wound up or otherwise dissolved or deregistered
  5. (e)become bankrupt
  6. (f)are expelled under clause 20,
  7. (g)become mentally incapacitated or become a person or estate liable to be dealt with in any way under laws relating to mental health, or
  8. (h)die.

19–20Dispute resolution and disciplinary procedures

19Dispute resolution

19.1Application

This procedure applies to disputes under this constitution between a member or director and:

  1. (a)one or more members
  2. (b)one or more directors, or
  3. (c)the company itself.

A member must not start a dispute resolution procedure in relation to a matter which is the subject of a disciplinary procedure under clause 20, until that disciplinary procedure is completed.

19.2Notice of dispute resolution

Those involved in the dispute must try to resolve it between themselves either within 14 days of becoming aware of it, or within a timeframe agreed on by those involved. If the dispute cannot be resolved within 14 days or within a timeframe agreed on by those involved, they must:

  1. (a)inform the company’s directors in writing
  2. (b)agree or request that a mediator be appointed, and
  3. (c)attempt in good faith to settle the dispute through mediation.

19.3Choosing a mediator

If those involved in the dispute cannot agree on the choice of mediator, then

  1. (a)for disputes between members, the directors will choose the mediator, or
  2. (b)for other disputes, the president of the law institute or society in the state or territory in which the company has its registered office will choose the mediator.

19.4Conditions of choosing a mediator

A mediator chosen by a director:

  1. (a)must not have a personal interest in the dispute, and
  2. (b)must be unbiased.

19.5During mediation

When conducting the mediation, the mediator must:

  1. (a)allow those involved a reasonable chance to be heard
  2. (b)allow those involved a reasonable chance to review any written statements
  3. (c)ensure the mediation is conducted in a manner free from bias, and
  4. (d)not make a decision on the dispute.

19.6Obligations of each party

Each party must:

  1. (a)do their best to resolve the dispute at the mediation conference, and
  2. (b)bear the costs of mediation as agreed between the parties.

The terms of any agreement reached between the parties must be provided to the company.

20Disciplining members

20.1Directors may discipline members

The directors may resolve to warn or expel a member who is not a director from the company if they believe:

  1. (a)the member has breached this constitution, or
  2. (b)the member’s behaviour is causing, has caused, or is likely to cause, harm to the company.

20.2Notification of action

At least 14 days before the directors’ meeting at which such a resolution will be considered, the secretary must notify the member in writing:

  1. (a)that the directors are considering a resolution to warn or expel the member
  2. (b)that this resolution will be considered at a directors’ meeting, the date of which must also be provided
  3. (c)the claims made against the member
  4. (d)the nature of the resolution that is to be proposed at the meeting, and
  5. (e)that the member may provide an explanation to the directors, as well as how the member can do so.

20.3Member must be given opportunity to explain or defend themselves

Before the directors pass any resolution, the member must be given a chance to explain or defend themselves by:

  1. (a)sending the directors a written explanation prior to that directors’ meeting
  2. (b)speaking at the meeting, or
  3. (c)both.

20.4Directors’ rights – disciplining members

After considering any explanation provided, the directors must decide to:

  1. (a)take no further action
  2. (b)warn the member
  3. (c)expel the member
  4. (d)require the matter to be determined at a general meeting, or
  5. (e)refer the decision to an unbiased, independent person on conditions that the directors consider appropriate. This independent person can only make a decision that the directors themselves can make under this clause.

20.5Other

  1. (a)The secretary must give written notice to the member of the directors’ decision as soon as possible.
  2. (b)The directors cannot fine a member.
  3. (c)Disciplinary procedures must be completed as soon as reasonably possible.
  4. (d)There will be no liability for any loss or injury the member suffers due to any decision made in good faith under this clause.

21–30General meetings of members

21Accountability to members

The company must hold an annual general meeting within 18 months after its incorporation and thereafter within five months after the end of each financial year, in accordance with the Corporations Act. At each annual general meeting, the directors must present the company's financial statements and a report on its activities for the preceding financial year.

The directors may also call a general meeting at any time.

22Members can request a general meeting

22.1How members can request a general meeting

Members, acting for a proper purpose, can request that the directors call a general meeting, by sending a written request to the company that:

  1. (a)states any resolution to be proposed at the meeting, and
  2. (b)is signed, by at least 5% of members who can cast votes at a general meeting (calculated as of midnight the day before the meeting is requested).

22.2Directors must then hold a general meeting

The directors must:

  1. (a)within 21 days of the members’ request, give all members notice of a general meeting, and
  2. (b)hold the general meeting within two months of the members’ request.

22.3If the directors do not call a requested meeting

  1. (a)If the directors do not call the meeting within 21 days of being requested to under clause 22.1, 50% or more of the members who made the request may call a general meeting.
  2. (b)The members must:
    1. i.as far as possible, follow the procedures for a general meeting as set out in this constitution
    2. ii.call the meeting using the list of members on the company’s member register, which the company must provide to members making the request at no cost, and
    3. iii.hold the general meeting within three months of the request being given to the company.
  3. (c)The company must reimburse the members who request the general meeting for any reasonable expenses they incur because the directors did not call and hold the meeting.

23Using technology to hold meetings

23.1A virtual meeting platform may be used

The company may hold a hybrid or virtual-only general meeting using an appropriate virtual meeting platform.

23.2If the general meeting is virtual only

If the general meeting is held using only a virtual meeting platform, then

  1. (a)the meeting location is taken to be the registered office of the company, and
  2. (b)the meeting time is taken to be the time at the company’s registered office.

23.3If the general meeting is hybrid only

If the general meeting is held at more than one physical venue (whether or not it is also held using a virtual meeting platform), then:

  1. (a)the meeting location is taken to be the main physical venue of the meeting, as set out in the notice of the meeting, and
  2. (b)the meeting time is taken to be the time at the main physical venue of the meeting, as set out in the notice of the meeting.

24Providing notice of a general meeting

24.1When notice of a general meeting must be given

The notice of a general meeting must be given at least 21 days before the meeting to:

  1. (a)each member entitled to vote at the meeting
  2. (b)each director, and
  3. (c)the auditor (if applicable).

24.2When short notice of a general meeting can be given

The notice of a general meeting may be provided less than 21 days before the meeting date if members with at least 75% of the votes that may be cast at the meeting agree beforehand, but not if a resolution will be moved to:

  1. (a)remove a director
  2. (b)appoint a director to replace a director who has been removed, or
  3. (c)remove an auditor, if applicable.

24.3What the notice must include

The notice of a general meeting must be in writing and include:

  1. (a)the place, date and time for the meeting (and if the meeting is to be held in two or more places, hybrid or virtually, the virtual meeting platform that will be used)
  2. (b)a statement regarding the member’s right to request documents be sent to them in electronic or physical form or request to not be sent documents
  3. (c)the general nature of the meeting’s business
  4. (d)if applicable, that a special resolution will be proposed, as well as the wording of the proposed resolution, and
  5. (e)a statement that members have the right to appoint proxies and that, if a member appoints a proxy:
    1. i.the proxy does not need to be a member
    2. ii.the proxy form must be delivered to the company at either its registered address or the address (including an electronic address) specified in the notice of the meeting, and
    3. iii.the proxy form must be delivered to the company at least 48 hours before the meeting.

24.4Meeting or notice not invalidated

A meeting or notice of meeting is not invalidated only because of the accidental omission to give notice of the meeting or the non-receipt by any person of notice of the meeting.

25Quorum at general meetings

25.1What is a quorum

For a general meeting to be held, a quorum must be present (in person, by proxy or by representative). A quorum is the greater of three members or 10% of the total membership, except that if the total membership is fewer than three, all members must be present.

25.2How is a quorum calculated

When determining if there is a quorum, a person may only be counted once. If they are a representative or proxy of more than one member, they may be counted as present for each of the different members.

25.3Quorum must be present

No business can be conducted at a general meeting if there is not a quorum present. A quorum must be present for the whole general meeting.

25.4If there is no quorum

If there is no quorum present within 30 minutes after the starting time stated in the notice of general meeting, the general meeting is adjourned to a date, time and place of the chair’s choosing. If the chair does not specify one or more of those things, the meeting is adjourned to:

  1. (a)if no date is specified – the same day in the next week
  2. (b)if no time is specified – the same time, and
  3. (c)if no venue or virtual meeting platform is specified – the same venue and virtual meeting platform.

If no quorum is present at this resumed meeting within 30 minutes after the starting time set for the meeting, then the meeting is cancelled.

26Right of non-members to attend meetings

  1. (a)The chair of a general meeting may invite any person to attend and address a general meeting.
  2. (b)If the company has an auditor, then the auditor must be given any communications relating to the general meeting that a member is entitled to receive. The auditor is also entitled to attend and address a general meeting, including by sending a written statement.

27Representatives of members

27.1Incorporated members may appoint a representative

An incorporated member may appoint as a representative:

  1. (a)one individual to represent the member at meetings and to sign and agree to resolutions under clause 32, and
  2. (b)the same individual or another individual for the purpose of being appointed or elected as a director.

27.2How to appoint a representative

The appointment of a representative by a member must:

  1. (a)be in writing
  2. (b)include the name of the person
  3. (c)be signed on behalf of the member
  4. (d)confirm the length of the appointment, which may be for a set number of meetings, or for a duration of time or standing (ongoing)
  5. (e)be given to the company or, for representation at a meeting, be given to the chair before the meeting starts, and
  6. (f)be recorded in the register of members.

27.3Rights of representative

A representative has all the rights of a member relevant to the purposes of the appointment (during any relevant timeframe) as a representative, including to appoint a proxy.

28Choosing a chair for a general meeting

The chairperson is entitled to chair general meetings.

The members present who are entitled to vote at a general meeting may choose a director or member to be the chair for that meeting if:

  1. (a)there is no chairperson, or
  2. (b)the chairperson is not present within 30 minutes after the starting time set for the meeting, or
  3. (c)the chairperson is present but does not wish to act as the meeting’s chairperson.

29Role of the chair in a general meeting

The chair is responsible for the conduct of the general meeting and must give members a reasonable opportunity to make comments and ask questions (including to the auditor if there is one).

The chair does not have a casting vote.

30Adjournment of meetings

  1. (a)If a quorum is present, a general meeting must be adjourned if the majority of members in attendance direct the chair to do so.
  2. (b)Only unfinished business may be dealt with at a meeting resumed after an adjournment.
  3. (c)If a general meeting is adjourned for one month or more, the members must be given new notice of the resumed meeting.

31–32Members’ resolutions and statements

31Members may propose resolutions and give statements

31.1When can members propose resolutions and give statements

Members with at least 5% of the votes that may be cast on a resolution may give:

  1. (a)written notice signed by the members to the company of a resolution they propose to move for a proper purpose at a general meeting (members’ resolution), with the wording of the resolution set out in full
  2. (b)a written request to the company that the company give all its members a statement about a proposed resolution or any other matter that may properly be considered at a general meeting (members’ statement), with the wording of the statement set out in full, or
  3. (c)both.

The calculation for the percentage of votes that members have is to be based on the situation as of midnight before the request or notice is given to the company.

31.2Members’ resolutions must be considered

If the company has been given notice of a members' resolution under clause 31.1, the resolution must be considered at the next general meeting held no more than two months after the notice is given.

31.3Distributing notice of proposed resolution and statement

The company must give all its members notice of the members’ resolution or a copy of the members’ statement at the same time, or as soon as practicable afterwards, and in the same way as it gives notice of a general meeting.

31.4Who bears the costs of giving notice or distributing statement

  1. (a)The company is responsible for the cost of giving members notice of the resolution or a copy of the members’ statement if the company receives it in time to send it out to members with the notice of meeting.
  2. (b)If the company does not receive it in time to send it out with the notice of meeting, then the members requesting the meeting must meet the reasonable expenses that the company incurs when giving members notice of the members’ resolution or a copy of the members’ statement. At a general meeting, the members may pass a resolution that the company will pay these expenses.

31.5When does the company not need to send notice or distribute statement

The company does not need to send the notice of proposed members’ resolution or a copy of the members' statement to members if:

  1. (a)it is more than 1,000 words long
  2. (b)the directors consider that it may be defamatory
  3. (c)the members making the request are to bear the expenses of sending the notice out—unless the members give the company a sum that would meet all reasonable expenses that it would incur in giving the notice, or
  4. (d)in the case of a proposed members’ resolution, the resolution does not relate to a matter that may be properly considered at a general meeting or is otherwise not a valid resolution able to be put to the members.

32Resolutions of members without a general meeting

32.1When a resolution may be passed without a general meeting

The directors may put a resolution to the members to be passed without a general meeting being held. However, the company cannot pass resolutions for the following without holding a meeting:

  1. (a)a resolution to remove an auditor or remove a director, or
  2. (b)where the Corporations Act or this constitution requires a meeting to be held.

32.2When is a resolution passed

A resolution is passed if 75% of the members entitled to vote on the resolution sign or agree to the resolution.

32.3How a resolution may be passed

The members may sign in the way set out in clause 5. Alternatively, the company may send a resolution by email to members, and members may agree to it by sending a reply email to that effect and including the text of the resolution in their reply.

32.4Notifying the auditor

The company must notify the auditor (if applicable) as soon as possible that a resolution has or will be put to members, and set out the wording of the resolution.

33–34Voting at general meetings

33Voting at general meetings

33.1Number of votes for each member

Each member has one vote.

33.2Challenge to member’s right to vote

A member or the chair may only challenge a person’s right to vote at the time of the general meeting. The chair must then decide whether the person may vote. The chair’s decision is final.

33.3How voting is conducted

Before a vote is taken, the chair must note whether any proxy votes with a voting direction have been received and, if so, how the proxy votes will be cast.

Voting must be conducted and decided by:

  1. (a)a show of hands
  2. (b)a vote in writing, or
  3. (c)another method chosen by the chair that is fair and reasonable in the circumstances.

33.4When a resolution is passed

  1. (a)A resolution (other than a special resolution) is passed if at least 50% of the total votes are in favour of the resolution being passed.
  2. (b)If there is a vote conducted by a show of hands, the chair’s decision is conclusive evidence of the result of the vote by show of hands. The chair and the meeting minutes do not need to state the number or proportion of the votes recorded in favour or against on a show of hands.

33.5When and how a vote in writing may be demanded

A vote in writing may be demanded on any resolution instead of, or after, a vote by a show of hands by:

  1. (a)at least five members present who are entitled to vote on the relevant resolution
  2. (b)members present with at least 5% of the votes that may be passed on the relevant resolution (worked out as at the midnight before the vote in writing is demanded), or
  3. (c)the chair of the meeting.

33.6When and how a vote in writing must be held

A vote in writing must be conducted at the chair’s direction, but must be held immediately if it is demanded for the following:

  1. (a)the election of a chair under clause 28, or
  2. (b)to decide whether to adjourn the meeting.

33.7Withdrawing

A demand for a vote in writing may be withdrawn.

34Proxies

34.1Appointment of proxy

A member may appoint a proxy to attend and vote at a general meeting on their behalf. The member can decide how long the appointment is for. For example, it can be for a set number of meetings, or for a duration of time or standing (ongoing).

A proxy does not need to be a member.

34.2What a proxy can and can’t do

A proxy appointed to attend and vote for a member has the same rights as the member to:

  1. (a)speak at the meeting
  2. (b)vote in writing (but only in the manner allowed by the appointment), and
  3. (c)demand a vote in writing under clause 33.5.

A proxy is not entitled to vote on a show of hands (but this does not prevent a member appointed as a proxy from voting as a member on a show of hands).

A proxy does not have the authority to speak and vote for a member at a meeting while the member is at the meeting in person or by technology.

34.3How a proxy votes

When a vote in writing is held, a proxy:

  1. (a)does not need to vote, unless the proxy appointment specifies the way they must vote
  2. (b)must vote in the way specified on the proxy form, if applicable, and
  3. (c)may cast votes in different ways (if the proxy is also a member or holds more than one proxy).

34.4How to appoint a proxy

An appointment of a proxy (by using a proxy form) must be signed by the member appointing the proxy and must contain:

  1. (a)the member’s name and address
  2. (b)the company’s name
  3. (c)the proxy’s name or the name of the office held by the proxy, and
  4. (d)the length of the appointment, measured as a set number of meetings, or for a duration of time or standing (ongoing).

Proxy forms must be delivered to the address specified in the notice under clause 24.3(e) at least 48 hours before a meeting. The chair may accept late notices.

34.5Validity

Unless the company receives written notice before the start or resumption of a general meeting at which a proxy votes, a vote cast by the proxy is valid even if, before the proxy votes, the appointing member:

  1. (a)revokes the proxy’s appointment
  2. (b)revokes the authority of a representative or agent who appointed the proxy
  3. (c)is mentally incapacitated, or
  4. (d)dies.

35–44Directors

35Number of directors

The company must have at least three and no more than nine directors.

36Who can be a director

An individual is eligible to be a company director if they:

  1. (a)are, or agree to become, a member of the company (and if not already a member, the directors may admit that individual as a member upon or before their appointment taking effect)
  2. (b)are 18 years of age or older
  3. (c)have a director identification number
  4. (d)give the company their signed consent to act as a company director, and
  5. (e)are not ineligible to be a director under the Corporations Act or the ACNC Act.

37How to become a director

  1. (a)The initial directors are the individuals who have agreed to act as directors and who are named as proposed directors in the application for company registration.
  2. (b)Subject to clause 35, the members may elect a director by passing a resolution. Each election must be by separate resolution.

38The directors may appoint a director

The directors may appoint an individual as a director to fill a casual vacancy or as an additional director, if that individual meets the eligibility requirements set out in clause 36.

39What happens if there are too few directors

If the number of directors is reduced to fewer than three or is less than the number required for a quorum, the continuing directors may act but only:

  1. (a)in an emergency
  2. (b)for the purpose of increasing the number of directors to three or the number required to form a quorum, or
  3. (c)to call a general meeting.

40Election of chairperson

The directors must elect a director as the company’s chairperson and may decide the period for which that person is to be chairperson.

The chairperson’s responsibilities include:

  1. (a)managing board meetings, including setting the agenda
  2. (b)ensuring directors receive accurate, timely, and clear information
  3. (c)ensuring effective communication with members, and
  4. (d)managing disputes to ensure the company is run effectively.

41Term of office

  1. (a)Elected directors hold office for a term of two years and are eligible for re-election for further terms of two years each, with no maximum service limit, unless the members resolve by special resolution to set a limit for a particular director.
  2. (b)Directors appointed to fill a casual vacancy hold that office until the end of the term that would have been served by the former director they are replacing.

42How to stop being a director

A director stops being a director if they:

  1. (a)resign in writing to the secretary
  2. (b)stop being a member
  3. (c)are a representative of a member, and that member stops being a member
  4. (d)are a representative of a member, and the member notifies the company that the representative is no longer a representative
  5. (e)are removed from office under clause 43
  6. (f)are appointed for a term of office and are not reappointed
  7. (g)are disqualified from being a director under the Corporations Act or the ACNC Act, or
  8. (h)die.

43How to remove a director

The members may remove a director by passing a resolution at a general meeting. Clause 31 does not apply. Instead, the process is as follows:

  1. (a)The members must provide notice of intention to move a members’ resolution to remove a director at least 2 months before the meeting is to be held.
  2. (b)The company must provide a copy of the notice to the director as soon as practicable after it is received.
  3. (c)The director can give the company a written statement to circulate to the members and speak to the motion at the meeting.
  4. (d)The members must pass a resolution to remove the director.

44How to appoint and remove an auditor

The members may appoint an auditor by passing a resolution at a general meeting. The members may remove an auditor by passing a resolution in a general meeting.

45–49Powers of directors

45Powers of directors

45.1Powers

  1. (a)The directors may exercise all the powers of the company except any powers that, under the Corporations Act or this constitution, may only be used by members.
  2. (b)The directors may borrow or raise money, charge any property or business of the company or give any other security for a debt, liability or obligation of the company.

45.2Responsibilities

The directors are responsible for managing and directing the activities of the company to carry out the purpose(s) set out in clause 7.

The directors must decide on the responsible financial management of the company including:

  1. (a)any suitable written delegations of power under clause 46, and
  2. (b)how money will be managed – including how electronic transfers must be authorised, signed off or otherwise approved.

45.3Limitations

The directors cannot remove a director or auditor. Directors and auditors may only be removed by a resolution passed at a general meeting.

46Delegation of directors’ powers

46.1Directors may delegate powers

The directors may delegate any of their powers and functions to:

  1. (a)a committee
  2. (b)a particular director
  3. (c)a company employee (such as a chief executive officer) or
  4. (d)any other person, as they consider it appropriate to do so.

46.2Record-keeping

The company must keep appropriate records of any delegations of powers.

47Payments to directors

The company must not pay fees for acting as a director (also called sitting fees). This does not stop the company from making the following types of payments:

  1. (a)paying a director for work they do for the company other than as a director, or
  2. (b)reimbursing a director for expenses they’ve properly incurred for work in connection with the company’s affairs.

Any payment made under this clause must be no more than is fair and reasonable to the company and approved by the directors in accordance with clause 52.

48Execution of documents

The company may execute a document if the document is signed in accordance with clause 5 by:

  1. (a)two directors of the company
  2. (b)a director and the secretary, or
  3. (c)an individual or a combination of individuals authorised by the board for that purpose.

49Validity

An act carried out by a director, or by a meeting of directors, or by a committee attended by a director, is not invalid just because:

  1. (a)of a defect in the appointment of the director
  2. (b)the individual is disqualified from being a director or has vacated office, or
  3. (c)the individual is not entitled to vote,

if that circumstance was not known by the individual or the directors or committee, when the act was carried out.

50–56Duties of directors

50Governance Standards

The company must ensure that it complies with the ACNC Governance Standards and, if applicable, ACNC External Conduct Standards.

51Duties of directors

The directors must comply with their duties:

  1. (a)to exercise their powers and discharge their duties with the degree of care and diligence that a reasonable individual would exercise if they were a director of the company
  2. (b)to act in good faith in the best interests of the company, and to further the charitable purpose(s) of the company set out in clause 7
  3. (c)to not misuse their position as a director
  4. (d)to not misuse information they gain in their role as a director
  5. (e)to disclose any perceived or actual material conflicts of interest in the manner set out in clause 52
  6. (f)to ensure that the financial affairs of the company are managed responsibly, and
  7. (g)to not allow the company to operate while it is insolvent.

52Conflicts of interest

A director who has a perceived or actual material conflict of interest in a company matter must let the other directors know.

The perceived or actual material conflict of interest, including the nature and extent of the interest and the relationship of the interest to the company, must also be recorded in the register of interests.

Each director who has a material personal interest in a matter must not:

  1. (a)be present at the meeting while the matter is being discussed, or
  2. (b)vote on the matter,

unless

  1. (c)their interest arises because they are a member of the company, and the other members have the same interest
  2. (d)their interest relates to an insurance contract that insures, or would insure, the director against liabilities that the director incurs as a director of the company (see clause 79)
  3. (e)their interest relates to a payment by the company under clause 78 (indemnity), or any contract relating to an indemnity that is allowed under the Corporations Act
  4. (f)the Australian Securities and Investments Commission (ASIC) makes an order allowing the director to vote on the matter, or
  5. (g)the directors who do not have a material personal interest in the matter pass a resolution that:
    1. i.identifies the director, the nature and extent of the director’s interest in the matter and how it relates to the affairs of the company, and
    2. ii.says that those directors are satisfied that the interest should not stop the director from voting or being present.

If a majority of directors have a material personal interest in a matter such that a quorum of unconflicted directors cannot be formed, the directors must refer the matter to the members to be decided by ordinary resolution at a general meeting, or by resolution of the members without a meeting under clause 32, unless the matter is urgent, in which case the directors who are not conflicted may act to the minimum extent necessary to protect the interests of the company, and must refer the matter to members as soon as practicable thereafter.

53Investment policy

The directors must adopt, maintain, and regularly review a written investment policy that governs the management and investment of the company's endowment assets. The investment policy must:

  1. (a)be consistent with the company's charitable purposes as set out in clause 7
  2. (b)reflect the prudent investor standard, having regard to the long-term nature of the endowment
  3. (c)specify the asset allocation, risk parameters, and performance benchmarks for the endowment fund, and
  4. (d)be reviewed by the directors at least once every two years.

The directors may engage professional investment advisers to assist in the management of the endowment fund, provided that the directors retain oversight and accountability for all investment decisions.

54Corpus protection and distribution discretion

The company operates as a permanent endowment fund. The corpus of the endowment fund must not be distributed or expended under any circumstances, and must be preserved intact for the long-term pursuit of the company's charitable purposes.

The directors must, in each financial year, consider whether to make grants or distributions from the income and returns of the endowment fund. The directors may resolve not to make any distribution in a financial year where, in their reasonable judgement:

  1. (a)the income and returns generated by the endowment fund in that year are insufficient to support a meaningful distribution after meeting the company's operating costs; or
  2. (b)it is in the long-term interests of the endowment fund and the company's charitable purposes to reinvest returns rather than distribute them.

The directors must document their consideration of this requirement, and the reasons for any decision not to distribute, in the minutes of the relevant directors' meeting.

55Grants policy

The directors must adopt, maintain, and regularly review a written grants policy that governs all grant-making activities of the company. The grants policy must:

  1. (a)set out the eligibility criteria for grant recipients, including that grants must further the purposes in clause 7
  2. (b)require due diligence to be conducted on all proposed recipients, with enhanced due diligence for overseas recipients
  3. (c)specify approval thresholds, including that grants above a threshold set by the directors must be approved by a resolution of the board
  4. (d)require written grant agreements for all grants above a threshold set by the directors, and
  5. (e)require records to be kept of all grant applications received, decisions made, and grants paid.

The grants policy must be reviewed by the directors at least once every two years.

56Overseas operations governance

Given that the company's purposes are directed at activities overseas, the directors must adopt, maintain, and regularly review the following written policies to ensure compliance with the ACNC External Conduct Standards:

  1. (a)a risk management framework that identifies and manages the risks of operating and making grants in Sri Lanka
  2. (b)a fraud and corruption control policy covering the company's overseas operations and grant-making activities, and
  3. (c)a safeguarding policy to protect vulnerable individuals who may be affected by the company's overseas activities.
  4. (d)a serious incidents and complaints policy that:
  5. (e)establishes a process for receiving, recording, and responding to complaints from members, grant recipients, beneficiaries, and the public
  6. (f)requires the directors to report serious incidents (including fraud, significant financial loss, and harm to individuals) to the ACNC in accordance with the ACNC's serious incident reporting requirements, and
  7. (g)protects individuals who raise complaints or report incidents in good faith from any adverse action by the company
  8. (h)The directors must ensure that the company's overseas partners and grant recipients are made aware of, and agree to operate consistently with, these policies.

57–60Directors’ meetings

57Directors’ meetings

57.1When the directors meet

The directors may decide how, where, when and how often they meet.

57.2Calling directors’ meetings

A director may call a directors’ meeting by giving reasonable notice to all other directors, or by the secretary giving reasonable notice of the meeting to all directors. The notice can be in writing or by any other means of communication.

57.3Chairperson for directors’ meetings

The chairperson must chair directors’ meetings.

The directors at a directors’ meeting may choose another director to chair that meeting if the chairperson:

  1. (a)is not present within 30 minutes after the starting time set for the meeting, or
  2. (b)is present but does not want to act as chairperson of the meeting.

Where the votes on a proposed resolution are equal, the chair of the meeting does not have a second or casting vote, and the vote is taken as lost.

57.4What is a quorum

The quorum for a directors’ meeting is the presence of a majority of directors and must always be present during a directors’ meeting.

57.5If a quorum cannot be maintained

  1. (a)If a directors’ meeting cannot maintain a quorum due to one or more directors having a conflict of interest that prevents them from being present when a matter is discussed, directors may put the matter to the members at the next general meeting, or at an earlier time if it is reasonable to do so.
  2. (b)The members can pass an ordinary resolution to deal with the matter.

58Using technology to hold directors’ meetings

The directors may hold their meetings (including hybrid meetings) by using any virtual meeting platform.

A director may only withdraw their consent to use the agreed-to platform within a reasonable period before the meeting.

59Passing directors’ resolutions at a meeting

A directors’ resolution must be passed by a majority of the votes cast by directors present and entitled to vote on the resolution.

60Resolutions of directors without a meeting

A resolution can be passed without a meeting if all the directors entitled to vote on the resolution sign or otherwise agree to the resolution by:

  1. (a)the method set out in clause 5, or
  2. (b)confirming their agreement by reply email to the company within five business days.

The resolution is passed when the last director signs or otherwise agrees to the resolution.

61–63Secretary

61Appointment of secretary

  1. (a)The company must have at least one secretary, who may also be a director.
  2. (b)The secretary must be at least 18 years old.
  3. (c)The secretary must give the company their signed consent to become a secretary before being appointed by directors.
  4. (d)The directors must decide the terms and conditions under which the secretary is appointed, including any remuneration.
  5. (e)The initial secretary is the individual who has agreed to act as a secretary and who is named as a proposed secretary in the application for company registration.

62Role of secretary

The directors may delegate the following to the secretary:

  1. (a)maintaining a register of the company’s members (see clause 12)
  2. (b)maintaining the minutes and other records of general meetings (including notices of meetings), directors’ meetings and resolutions, and
  3. (c)notifying the ACNC of applicable changes, such as the resignation or appointment of a director, change of address, or change to the constitution.

63How to stop being a secretary

The secretary stops being secretary if they:

  1. (a)resign in writing to the company
  2. (b)are removed by resolution of the directors
  3. (c)are disqualified from managing corporations under the Corporations Act or the ACNC Act, or
  4. (d)die.

64–65Financial matters

64Funds

The company’s money must be deposited into a company bank account. The directors may decide to maintain a ‘petty cash’ fund for minor and incidental expenses. Withdrawals or deposits from the petty cash fund must be recorded.

65Company's financial year

The company's financial year is from 1 July to 30 June.

66–73Minutes and records

66Records of members’ meetings

The company must, within one month, make and keep the following records:

  1. (a)minutes of proceedings and resolutions of general meetings
  2. (b)minutes of any other resolutions of members
  3. (c)a copy of a notice of each general meeting, and
  4. (d)a copy of any members’ statement distributed to members under clause 31.3.

67Records of directors’ meetings

The company must, within one month, make and keep the following records:

  1. (a)minutes of proceedings and resolutions of directors’ meetings
  2. (b)minutes of proceedings and resolutions of meetings of any committees, and
  3. (c)minutes of any other resolutions of directors.

68Signing minutes

68.1Minutes of a meeting

The directors must ensure that minutes of a general meeting or a directors’ meeting are signed in accordance with clause 5 and by:

  1. (a)the chair of the meeting, or
  2. (b)the chair of the next meeting.

68.2Minutes of resolutions without meetings

The directors must ensure that the passing of a resolution without a meeting (of members or directors) is recorded and signed by a director within a reasonable time after the resolution is passed.

69Financial and related records

The company must make and keep written financial records that:

  1. (a)correctly record and explain its transactions and financial position and performance, and
  2. (b)enable true and fair financial statements to be prepared and to be audited.

The company must also keep written records that correctly record its operations.

Records may be kept in physical or electronic format.

70Inspecting the register of members

The company must allow members (and non-members upon payment of a fee) to inspect the register of members. To inspect the register, the person must submit their request in writing with their name, address and the purpose of their request.

Information that is accessed from the register of members must only be used in a manner relevant to the interests or rights of members as members or in a manner approved by the company.

71Inspection of records

  1. (a)The company must give a member access to the records set out in clause 66.
  2. (b)The directors may give a member access to other records of the company. The directors may choose to provide limited access or redacted copies of these records.

72How long records must be kept for

  1. (a)The company must retain its records for at least seven years.
  2. (b)The directors must take reasonable steps to ensure that the company's records are kept safe.

73Directors’ access to documents

  1. (a)A director has a right to access the company’s financial records at all reasonable times.
  2. (b)The directors may resolve to give a director or former director access to other records, including documents provided for, or available to, the directors.

74–77Notice

74What is notice

Anything written to or from the company under any clause in this constitution is written notice and is subject to clauses 74 to 76 unless specified otherwise.

Clauses 74 to 76 do not apply to a notice of proxy under clause 34.4.

75Notice to the company

Any communication under this constitution may be given to the company, the directors or the secretary by:

  1. (a)delivering it to the company’s registered office
  2. (b)posting it to the company’s registered office or to another address the company chooses for notice to be provided to, or
  3. (c)sending it to an email address the company has notified members is the company’s email address.

76Notice to members

Written notice or any communication under this constitution may be given to a member:

  1. (a)in person
  2. (b)by sending it to the email address of the member (if any) nominated by the member for service of notices
  3. (c)by posting it to, or leaving it at, the postal address of the member (if any) nominated by the member for service of notices, or
  4. (d)by notifying the member via an email address they nominate that the notice is available for access via a website, in electronic form or at a specified place or address, if the member agrees to this method.

If a member elects to receive documents in physical form or electronic form, the company must take reasonable steps to send documents in the preferred manner.

77When notice is taken to be given

If a notice is:

  1. (a)delivered in person, or left at the recipient’s physical address, then it is taken to be given on the day it is delivered
  2. (b)sent by post, then it is taken to be given on the seventh business day after it is posted to the recipient’s address, and postage costs are paid for
  3. (c)sent by email, or other electronic method, then it is taken to be given when it is sent, unless the sender receives an automated message that the notice has not been delivered, or
  4. (d)given under clause 76(d), then it is taken to be given on the business day after notification of the notice being available is sent, unless the sender receives an automated message that the notice has not been delivered.

If the delivery or receipt of a notice is not on a business day, or is after 5pm on a business day, it is deemed to be received at 9am the following business day.

78–79Indemnity, insurance and directors’ access

78Indemnity

The company indemnifies its officers against any liability incurred in that capacity (other than to the company or a related body corporate), unless the liability did not arise out of conduct in good faith. In this clause and clause 79, ‘officer’ includes a director or secretary, and includes a director or secretary after they have stopped holding that office.

The indemnity is a continuing obligation and is enforceable by an officer:

  1. (a)even if that individual is no longer an officer, and
  2. (b)is enforceable without that individual first having to incur any expense or make any payment.

79Insurance

To the extent permitted by law (including the Corporations Act), and if the directors consider it appropriate, the company may pay a premium for a contract that insures a current or former company officer against any liability they incur in that role.

80–85Winding up

80Winding up voluntarily

If permitted by law, the members may pass a special resolution at a general meeting to wind up the company voluntarily.

81Surplus assets not to be distributed to members

If the company is wound up, any surplus assets must not be distributed to a member or a former member of the company, unless that member or former member is a charity which meets the requirements in clause 82.

82Distribution of surplus assets

82.1Distribution

Any surplus assets that remain on the winding up of the company must be distributed in accordance with the following order of preference:

  1. (a)first, to one or more registered Australian charities that operate an endowment fund with purposes similar to those of the company in clause 7 and which prohibit the distribution of surplus assets to their members; or, if no such organisation exists,
  2. (b)second, to one or more registered Australian charities whose purposes are similar to those of the company in clause 7 and which prohibit the distribution of surplus assets to their members.

82.2Making the decision

The specific charity or charities to receive the surplus assets must be identified in accordance with the order of preference in clause 82.1, by a special resolution of members at or before the time of winding up. If the members do not make this decision, the directors can do so. Failing that, the company may apply to the Supreme Court to make this decision.

83Liability of members limited to the guarantee

Each member must contribute an amount not more than $10 (the guarantee) to the property of the company if the company is wound up:

  1. (a)while they are a member, or within 12 months after they stop being a member, and
  2. (b)if at the time of winding up, the debts and liabilities the company incurred before they stopped being a member, exceed the company’s assets. These debts and liabilities also include any costs incurred in winding up the company.

The liability of each member is limited to the amount of the guarantee.

84Gift fund

84.1Establishing the fund

The company shall establish and maintain, for the purpose(s) of the company as set out in clause 7, a gift fund:

  1. (a)to which all gifts of money or property for the principal purpose of the company are to be made
  2. (b)to which contributions made in relation to an eligible fundraising event held for the principal purpose of the company are to be made
  3. (c)to which any money received by the company because of such gifts or contributions is to be credited, and
  4. (d)that does not receive any other money or property.

84.2Bank account

  1. (a)A separate bank account must be opened for the gift fund and only those items described in clause 84.1 must be deposited into that bank account.
  2. (b)Any monies deposited in the gift fund will be kept separate from all other funds of the company, will only be used to further the purpose(s) of the company as set out in clause 7, and must not be applied to meet the general operating or administrative expenses of the company except to the extent expressly permitted under the applicable DGR endorsement conditions.

85Endorsement as a deductible gift recipient

If the company is wound up or its endorsement as a deductible gift recipient is revoked (whichever occurs first), any surplus of the following assets shall be transferred to another organisation with similar objects, which is charitable at law, to which income tax deductible gifts can be made:

  1. (a)gifts of money or property for the principal purpose of the organisation
  2. (b)contributions made in relation to an eligible fundraising event held for the principal purpose of the organisation
  3. (c)money received by the organisation because of such gifts and contributions.
