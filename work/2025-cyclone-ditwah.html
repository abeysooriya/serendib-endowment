Cyclone Ditwah relief for 500 people

Five hundred displaced people supported after Cyclone Ditwah with books, dry rations and medicine, distributed through trusted local relationships in the Nuwara Eliya district.

Direct giving
Supported500 people
Support givenDry rations, books and medicine
WhereNuwara Eliya district

Cyclone Ditwah displaced families across the Nuwara Eliya district. The window in which help matters most after a cyclone is short, and it closes before most international appeals have finished fundraising.

Five hundred people were supported with dry rations, school books and medicine, staged from a local school hall and distributed by people who knew which households had lost what.

This is the case for an endowment in one photograph. Relief only works if the money is already there. A fund that has to raise before it can give arrives after the need has passed.

All of our work

The Serendib Endowment acknowledges the Turrbal and Jagera peoples, the Traditional Owners of Meanjin (Brisbane), the land on which we work, and their custodianship of these lands and waters over tens of thousands of years. As an organisation dedicated to supporting and uplifting our community, we draw inspiration from this long tradition of mutual care and stewardship. We pay our deepest respects to Elders past, present and emerging, and extend that respect to all Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples with us today.
